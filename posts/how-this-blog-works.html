How this blog works

No generator, no database, no build step. Just HTML files and stubbornness.

There's no static site generator, no build step, and no database. Every post is an HTML file in the posts/ folder, and the blog page is a list of links I update by hand, like it's 1998.

Writing a post

I copy an old post, rename it, and change the bits at the top:

  • The title, in both the <title> tag and the <h1>, because I will absolutely forget one of them.
  • The date and category above the title.
  • The one-line subtitle under it, which is usually where the joke goes.

Then I delete the old paragraphs and write new ones. That's the whole content management system.

Adding it to the blog

A new <li> goes at the top of the list in blog.html, with the same title, date, and category. A new category gets its own tab automatically. Each card gets one of six gradient covers, or a picture if I can be bothered to find one.

Sections and the sidebar

Every <h2> in a post shows up in the sidebar on wide screens, and it highlights the section you're reading, so you always know how far you are from being done. Posts with fewer than two headings don't get a sidebar, because a table of contents with one item is just a title.

Listening and sharing

Listen to article uses your browser's built-in voice to read the post aloud. Depending on your browser, it sounds anywhere from fine to a GPS reading a eulogy. The time next to it is a guess based on length. Share copies the link, or opens the share menu on a phone.

Then I push it to GitHub and it's live. Nothing to deploy, nothing to patch, nothing to page me about.
